Design Philosophy

🎯

Objectives first, always

Every design decision is anchored to a measurable learning objective. If it doesn't serve the outcome, it doesn't make the cut.

🧠

Learner-centered empathy

I conduct needs assessments and analyze learner data before touching a slide. Real design starts with real understanding.

📊

Measure and iterate

Training that can't be evaluated can't be improved. I build assessment and feedback loops into every deliverable.

⚡

Engagement as a strategy

Passive learners don't retain. I design for active participation — scenarios, reflection prompts, and purposeful interaction.

🏢 Corporate L&D · Case Study 01
New Employee Onboarding Redesign
Fictional client: Meridian Financial Group

The Problem

New hires reached their 90-day reviews without understanding core compliance requirements or culture. A needs assessment revealed 68% couldn't recall key compliance policies two weeks after orientation. The existing onboarding was a 4-hour slide deck read aloud by HR — no interaction, no assessment, no follow-up.

My Design Process

I conducted stakeholder interviews with HR, legal, and three department managers to identify the highest-priority gaps. Bloom's analysis revealed 90% of existing content operated only at the Remember level. I designed a 3-day blended solution: self-paced e-learning pre-work, two facilitated workshops, and a job aid library. I storyboarded, ran a pilot cohort, and revised before full rollout.

Outcomes & Impact

📉 30% faster time-to-productivity📋 12 role-specific job aids✅ Kirkpatrick L1 & L2 evaluation built in👥 2 pilot cohorts iterated
AudienceNew hires, all departments
Duration3-day blended program
ModalityILT + e-learning + job aids
ToolsArticulate Rise, Canvas LMS, Canva
FrameworkADDIE + Kirkpatrick
💻 E-Learning · Case Study 02
Adult Literacy: Writing for Everyday Life
City Literacy Project — 6-module self-paced course

The Problem

Community learners needed practical writing skills for real-life contexts. The existing course had a 30% completion rate. A learner survey identified the core barriers: content felt patronizing, reading level was inconsistent, and nothing was tied to scenarios learners actually recognized from their own lives.

My Design Process

I conducted 12 learner interviews before designing a single slide. Results showed scenario-based learning was the critical variable. I restructured the entire course around six real-life situations (cover letter, landlord email, workplace communication, etc.), applying plain language principles throughout. Every module was piloted with 3 learners before publishing.

Outcomes & Impact

📈 Completion: 30% → 70%🎯 6 scenario-based modules♿ Fully accessible design🎥 Loom video intros per module
AudienceAdult community learners
Duration6 self-paced modules (~25 min each)
ModalityAsynchronous e-learning
ToolsArticulate Rise, Loom, Google Sites
FrameworkADDIE + Adult Learning Theory
🎓 Professional Development · Case Study 03
Differentiated Instruction Workshop Series
Lincoln Unified School District — 8 sessions, 40+ educators

The Problem

District data showed inconsistent use of differentiated instruction across secondary classrooms. Previous PD had less than 20% application rate at 30 days. Teachers described sessions as irrelevant compliance exercises — content delivered at them, not with them. The format was the problem as much as the content.

My Design Process

I applied andragogy from the ground up: each session opened with a real classroom challenge from participants, modeled one specific strategy, provided structured practice time, and closed with a 2-week application commitment. I built peer observation and reflection cycles between sessions — designed into the sequence, not optional. I created all facilitator guides, participant workbooks, and slide decks from scratch.

Outcomes & Impact

⭐ 94% participant satisfaction📚 8 full facilitator guides🔁 Peer observation cycles built in✅ 80% applied 2+ strategies at 30 days
AudienceSecondary educators (40+)
Duration8 × 90-min ILT sessions
ModalityIn-person ILT + blended application
ToolsNearpod, Padlet, Google Slides
FrameworkADDIE + Andragogy + Kirkpatrick

Workplace Communication: Writing Clear Professional Emails

Sample lesson from the Meridian Financial Group Onboarding Program

Duration45 minutes
ModalityBlended (e-learning + discussion)
AudienceNew corporate hires
FrameworkADDIE + Bloom's Taxonomy

Learning Objectives

  • Identify the key components of a professional email Remember
  • Distinguish appropriate vs. inappropriate workplace tone Analyze
  • Compose a professional email response to a realistic scenario Create
  • Evaluate a peer's draft using a provided rubric Evaluate

Assessment Strategy

Formative
Scenario-based knowledge check

3 multiple-choice questions embedded in the module; learners must score 80% to proceed.

Summative
Email drafting task

Learners compose a reply to a simulated client complaint, assessed via a 4-point rubric covering tone, structure, clarity, and professionalism.

Instructional Sequence

1
Hook (5 min) — Email disaster case study

Learners read a poorly written email and its real-world consequences. Activates prior knowledge and primes motivation.

2
Instruction (10 min) — The CLEAR framework

Introduces a mnemonic: Concise, Logical, Error-free, Appropriate tone, Reader-focused.

3
Practice (15 min) — Spot & fix activity

Drag-and-drop interaction identifying issues in 3 sample emails with immediate feedback.

4
Application (10 min) — Compose a real email

Learners write a CLEAR-framework response to a simulated scenario for peer and facilitator review.

5
Debrief (5 min) — Reflection prompt

"What's one communication habit you want to change this week?" Shared in the cohort discussion board.

My Approach

ADDIE in practice

Every project follows a rigorous, iterative process. Here's what that looks like in the real world.

A

Analyze

Stakeholder interviews, learner surveys, performance gap analysis, and audience profiling before a single slide is designed.

D

Design

Learning objectives mapped to Bloom's levels, content outlines, storyboards, and assessment blueprints reviewed by SMEs.

D

Develop

Building modules in Articulate Rise or Storyline, creating facilitator guides, job aids, and multimedia assets.

I

Implement

Pilot testing with a small cohort, facilitating ILT sessions, deploying to LMS, and training facilitators on delivery.

E

Evaluate

Kirkpatrick Levels 1–3: learner reaction surveys, knowledge assessments, and 30-day behavior transfer checks.
